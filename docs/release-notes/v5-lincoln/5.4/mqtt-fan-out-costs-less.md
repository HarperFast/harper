### MQTT Fan-Out Costs Less

Delivering a message to many MQTT subscribers takes a fraction of the CPU it did: under half for exact topics and `+` wildcards, and a tenth or less for durable QoS 1/2 subscriptions. A burst of messages to one client is written to its socket in a few writes rather than one per message, on TCP connections and on WebSocket connections served by Node, and takes under a third of the CPU to deliver. A subscription holds about a third of the memory, durable session saves share transactions, and on RocksDB, many clients subscribing or resuming at once share the records they catch up with. On RocksDB, overlapping catch-ups also decode each stored record once between them rather than once each, which cuts a storm of subscribes over 1 KB records to less than half the CPU per record. Because saves are batched, two latencies can rise slightly, though the work as a whole finishes sooner: a PUBREL when one QoS 2 subscriber is sent messages as fast as possible (by about half a millisecond), and the slowest SUBACKs in a burst of thousands of durable subscribes (by a few milliseconds). A subscription event's `value` is read-only and can be the same frozen record object that other subscribers and `get()` receive, now for the records a subscription catches up with as well as for live changes; copy it before changing it.
